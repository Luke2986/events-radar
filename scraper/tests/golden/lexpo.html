Celebrating 10 Years of Legal Innovation

Lexpo’26 marks the 10-year anniversary of Europe’s leading legal innovation event. Since its launch in 2016, Lexpo has become the premier gathering for the Continental European legal community, bringing together forward-thinking law firms, in-house legal teams, and technology providers in Amsterdam.

On 8 & 9 June 2026, Lexpo celebrates a decade of shaping the future of the legal industry through innovation, legal technology, and digital transformation. This milestone edition will highlight both the promise of artificial intelligence and emerging technologies, and the central role of people in making digitalisation truly successful.

Lexpo’26 is about more than showcasing tools — it’s about building the culture, mindset, and strategic alignment needed for lasting digital transformation. Join us in Amsterdam for this milestone edition and be part of the next chapter in legal innovation.

Dutch Bar Association

Dutch lawyers can earn PO-points at Lexpo, contributing to the professional development requirements of the Dutch Bar Association (NOvA). Participants earn 1 PO-point per hour of sessions attended, excluding breaks. To ensure correct accreditation, attendees must sign in on arrival and sign out on departure. After the event, a Certificate of Attendance will be issued. The NOvA leaves it to lawyers to determine which Lexpo sessions contribute to their professional competence, allowing them to choose sessions that best support their development.

Lexpo'26 theme: The Human Advantage

In a legal innovation landscape defined by relentless technological advancement, one truth stands out: successful transformation depends on people, not products. That is why The Human Advantage is the central theme running through every session at Lexpo'26.

As AI, automation, and smarter tools become integral to legal work, the most significant differentiator is not the technology itself but the ability of organisations to adopt it effectively, integrate it into everyday practice, and lead their people through continual change. Across all sessions, Lexpo'26 explores how law firms and in-house teams can unlock the full potential of their people to drive meaningful, sustainable innovation.

Every conversation at Lexpo'26 returns to the mindsets, behaviours, and change-management capabilities required for technology adoption to succeed — how teams build trust in new systems, overcome resistance, reshape workflows, and collaborate across functions to embed new tools into the fabric of legal service delivery. And as AI accelerates routine tasks, the human skills that remain irreplaceable — empathy, critical thinking, judgement, communication — take centre stage throughout the programme.

From skills development and digital literacy to leadership at every level, Lexpo'26 is built around one conviction: the future of legal work depends not just on acquiring innovative tools, but on developing smarter, more adaptable teams who can adopt them well, evolve with them, and transform them into meaningful outcomes for clients.

Keynote

Cat Casey

The Human Edge: Leveraging Your Legal Superpowers in the Age of AI

November 30, 2022 was the moment AI stopped being theoretical and became unavoidable. The birth of ChatGPT pulled it out of research labs and dropped it into the real world overnight. Clients saw it. Courts saw it. Legal teams saw it. The conversation stopped being if AI would matter to law. It became who takes control of what happens next.

AI did not arrive politely. It arrived fast, persuasive, and increasingly confident. The loud narrative says AI is disrupting the law. The better read is that it is exposing where legal value has always lived. Modern AI is driven by language, structure, argument, and reasoning. That is the law’s home field. As execution gets cheaper, judgment becomes premium.

Here is the twist most of the industry still hasn’t fully owned. Legal professionals are not behind. AI has evolved to mirror how lawyers already think. Language. Ambiguity. Probabilistic reasoning. Lawyers live there. The real question is whether legal will recognise its advantage before someone else defines it for us.

This keynote is a challenge, not a comfort blanket. AI is already here. The question is how we direct it, how we pressure-test it, and how we set the standards before they are set for us. The law was built for this moment. The advantage is not adopting AI faster. It is taking the reins together, using AI as a force multiplier for legal thinking, and doubling down on the judgment that keeps our work trusted, defensible, and indispensable.

Session

Niki Avraam

The Broken Ladder: Lawyers Are Ready, Their Roles Aren’t

Law firms are investing heavily in technology. Productivity is rising. Yet something more fundamental is beginning to break.

For decades, the associate pyramid has relied on high-volume, repetitive work to develop lawyers: research, document review, drafting. That work is now being compressed or removed. Firms are redesigning workflows, but not the roles that sit within them.

The result is a structural gap. Lawyers are being asked to operate in a new model of delivery, while still sitting inside roles designed for the previous one.

Much of the conversation in the industry focuses on innovation capability, culture and adoption. This session looks at the layer underneath all three: how roles, career pathways and the allocation of work must change in practice.

Drawing on two decades inside law firms, Niki Avraam explores how leaders can redesign lawyer roles to match the way legal work is now delivered, moving from task execution to ownership of client problems, earlier responsibility and more fluid use of capability across teams.

The session introduces a practical approach to identifying where work is being compressed, what should replace it, and how firms can begin to rebuild development pathways as traditional learning structures disappear.

The firms that succeed will not simply adopt new tools.

They will redesign how their lawyers work.

Andrea Miskolczi

Andrea Miskolczi is the Founder and Director of InterAlia Consulting, a strategy and transformation advisory boutique for legal leaders. Over her 25-year career in the legal industry, Andrea has held leadership roles in, inter alia, legaltech, innovation, knowledge management, business development, marketing, and talent development with top-tier regional and global law firms. Before her foray into management, she worked as a transactional lawyer at Magic Circle law firms. Widely recognized in her field, Andrea frequently speaks and writes on topics such as applying AI technologies to legal workflows, change management, technology adoption, and the strategic challenges presented by a rapidly evolving multi-trend landscape. In 2022, she was shortlisted for the esteemed 'Innovative Lawyers' award in the Intrapreneur category by the Financial Times.

Frans Post

More about Frans

Frans Post

Frans is an experienced CFO and law firm expert who spent most of his career in boards of international professional service firms.

He worked for Clifford Chance for seventeen years, first in their Amsterdam office and later as Regional CFO for the Americas. From New York he moved to London where he held three C-suite roles at international firms. Frans then ran the finance function of offshore law firm Mourant in the Channel Islands. He returned to Amsterdam in 2021 to join De Brauw Blackstone Westbroek as their CFO. He is now a consultant and fractional CFO.

Frans has led finance strategy and operations, change projects, and several practice management system implementations. He is passionate about the use of data and business intelligence dashboards.

Frans has an Executive Master’s degree in Finance and Control. He is a Registered Controller (RC) and the Chairman of the Dutch Association of RC’s (VRC).
